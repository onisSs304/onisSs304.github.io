a closed-form expression of Riemannian metric, Riemannian gradient and exponential map for space of symmetric positive definite matrices with unit diagonal

Introduction

As discussed in the previous post, the space \( \mathrm{Cor}^+(n) \) admits a quotient structure, that is \( \mathrm{Cor}^+(n) \cong \mathrm{Sym}^+(n)/\mathrm{Diag}^+(n) \). The full proof can be found in [David & Gu, 2019]. Also in their work, to proceed the optimization procedure over this space, they used the same Riemannian metric (affine invariant) as for \( \mathrm{Sym}^+(n) \). However, in this post, we will try to derive another form of Riemannian metric under the effect of the quotient structure. The base theory we rely on comes from Chapter 9 of the book Introduction to Optimization on Smooth Manifolds by Nicolas Boumal. The flow of this post is:

  • Natural projection and its differential map
  • Vertical and horizontal spaces, then defining the horizontal lift
  • Derive the form of the Riemannian metric, Riemannian gradient, and exponential map
Natural Projection

As in the previous post, we define the natural projection from \( \mathrm{Sym}^+(n) \) to \( \mathrm{Cor}^+(n) \) as follows: \[ \pi(A) = \mathrm{Diag}(A)^{-1/2}\, A\, \mathrm{Diag}(A)^{-1/2}, \quad \forall\, A \in \mathrm{Sym}^+(n) \] Note that this projection defines a bijection between \( \mathrm{Cor}^+(n) \) and \( \mathrm{Cor}^+(n) \cong \mathrm{Sym}^+(n)/\mathrm{Diag}^+(n) \) and satisfies: \[ \pi(A) = \pi(DAD), \quad \forall\, A \in \mathrm{Sym}^+(n),\, D \in \mathrm{Diag}^+(n) \]

Differential map. Let \( A \in \mathrm{Sym}^+(n) \) and \( H \in T_A\mathrm{Sym}^+(n) \). The differential map \( D\pi(A) \) maps from \( T_A\mathrm{Sym}^+(n) \) to \( T_{\pi(A)}\mathrm{Cor}^+(n) \). Note that: \[ \forall\, K \in \mathrm{Cor}^+(n), \quad T_K\mathrm{Cor}^+(n) = \{H \in \mathrm{Sym}(n) \mid \mathrm{diag}(H) = 0_n\} \] Let \( f(A) = \mathrm{Diag}(A)^{-1/2} \). We have: \[ f(A + tH) = \mathrm{Diag}(A + tH)^{-1/2} = \mathrm{Diag}\!\left((A_{ii} + tH_{ii})^{-1/2}_{1 \leq i \leq n}\right) \] Then: \begin{align*} Df(A)[H] &= \frac{d}{dt} f(A + tH)\Big|_{t=0} = \mathrm{Diag}\!\left(\frac{d}{dt}(A_{ii} + tH_{ii})^{-1/2}_{1 \leq i \leq n}\right)\Big|_{t=0} \\ &= \mathrm{Diag}\!\left(-\tfrac{1}{2}A_{ii}^{-3/2}H_{ii}\right)_{1 \leq i \leq n} = -\tfrac{1}{2}\mathrm{Diag}(A)^{-3/2}\mathrm{Diag}(H) \end{align*} Therefore \( f(A + H) = f(A) - \frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Diag}(A)^{-3/2}\mathrm{Diag}(H) + O(H^2) \). Then: \begin{align*} \pi(A + H) &= f(A+H)(A+H)f(A+H) \\ &= \left(f(A) - \tfrac{1}{2}\mathrm{Diag}(A)^{-3/2}\mathrm{Diag}(H) + O(H^2)\right) (A+H) \left(f(A) - \tfrac{1}{2}\mathrm{Diag}(A)^{-3/2}\mathrm{Diag}(H) + O(H^2)\right) \\ &= \pi(A) + DHD - \tfrac{1}{2}D^3\mathrm{Diag}(H)AD - \tfrac{1}{2}DAD^3\mathrm{Diag}(H) + O(H^2) \end{align*} where \( D = \mathrm{Diag}(A)^{-1/2} \). Therefore: \[ D\pi(A)[H] = DHD - \tfrac{1}{2}D^3\mathrm{Diag}(H)AD - \tfrac{1}{2}DAD^3\mathrm{Diag}(H) \]

Vertical and Horizontal Spaces

Vertical space. According to Definition 9.24 in Boumal's book, the vertical space is defined at each \( A \in \mathrm{Sym}^+(n) \) as: \[ \mathcal{V}_A = \ker D\pi(A) = \{H \in \mathrm{Sym}(n) \mid D\pi(A)[H] = 0\} \] The differential map seems complicated, but we can compute the explicit form of the vertical space. Using the fact that two diagonal matrices commute, so \( D^3\mathrm{Diag}(H) = \mathrm{Diag}(H)D^3 \), we proceed as: \begin{align*} &DHD - \tfrac{1}{2}D^3\mathrm{Diag}(H)AD - \tfrac{1}{2}DAD^3\mathrm{Diag}(H) = 0 \\ &\iff H - \tfrac{1}{2}D^{-1}D^3\mathrm{Diag}(H)ADD^{-1} - \tfrac{1}{2}D^{-1}DAD^3\mathrm{Diag}(H)D^{-1} = 0 \\ &\iff H - \tfrac{1}{2}\mathrm{Diag}(H)D^2A - \tfrac{1}{2}AD^2\mathrm{Diag}(H) = 0 \\ &\iff H - \tfrac{1}{2}\mathrm{Diag}(H)\mathrm{Diag}(A)^{-1}A - \tfrac{1}{2}A\mathrm{Diag}(A)^{-1}\mathrm{Diag}(H) = 0 \end{align*} Taking equations entry by entry: \[ H_{ij} = \frac{1}{2}A_{ij}\!\left(\frac{H_{ii}}{A_{ii}} + \frac{H_{jj}}{A_{jj}}\right) \] Let \( s_{ii} = \frac{H_{ii}}{2A_{ii}} \), then \( H = AS + SA \) where \( S = \mathrm{Diag}(s) \). Finally, the vertical space is: \[ \mathcal{V}_A = \{AS + SA \mid S = \mathrm{Diag}(s),\, s \in \mathbb{R}^n\} \]

Horizontal space. The horizontal space is defined as: \[ \mathcal{H}_A = (\mathcal{V}_A)^\perp = \{K \in \mathrm{Sym}(n) \mid \langle K, H\rangle = 0,\, \forall\, H \in \mathcal{V}_A\} \] Since \( \mathrm{Sym}^+(n) \) is equipped with the affine invariant metric, we need to find: \[ \mathcal{H}_A = \{K \in \mathrm{Sym}(n) \mid \mathrm{Tr}(A^{-1}KA^{-1}H) = 0,\, \forall\, H \in \mathcal{V}_A\} \] Substituting \( H = AS + SA \): \begin{align*} \mathrm{Tr}(A^{-1}KA^{-1}(SA + AS)) &= \mathrm{Tr}(A^{-1}KA^{-1}SA) + \mathrm{Tr}(A^{-1}KA^{-1}AS) \\ &= \mathrm{Tr}(KA^{-1}S) + \mathrm{Tr}(A^{-1}KS) \\ &= \mathrm{Tr}((KA^{-1} + A^{-1}K)S) = 0 \end{align*} This holds iff \( \mathrm{diag}(KA^{-1} + A^{-1}K) = 0 \). Since \( A^{-1} \) and \( K \) are symmetric, \( \mathrm{diag}(KA^{-1}) = \mathrm{diag}(A^{-1}K) \). So the horizontal space is: \[ \mathcal{H}_A = \{K \in \mathrm{Sym}(n) \mid \mathrm{diag}(KA^{-1}) = 0\} \]

Horizontal lift. Let \( A \in \mathrm{Sym}^+(n) \) and \( V \in T_{\pi(A)}\mathrm{Cor}^+(n) \). The horizontal lift \( \mathrm{lift}_A(V) \) of \( V \) at \( A \) is the element in \( \mathcal{H}_A \) such that \( D\pi(A)[\mathrm{lift}_A(V)] = V \), i.e.: \[ \mathrm{lift}_A(V) = (D\pi(A)|_{\mathcal{H}_A})^{-1}[V] \] In our situation, this is equivalent to solving: \[ D\pi(A)[H] = DHD - \tfrac{1}{2}D^3\mathrm{Diag}(H)AD - \tfrac{1}{2}DAD^3\mathrm{Diag}(H) = V \] with \( D = \mathrm{Diag}(A)^{-1/2} \) and \( H \in \mathcal{H}_A \). We have \( \mathrm{diag}(V) = 0_n \). A key observation: if \( A \in \mathrm{Cor}^+(n) \), then: \[ D\pi(A)[V] = \mathrm{Diag}(A)^{-1/2}V\,\mathrm{Diag}(A)^{-1/2} = V \] Following this, if \( A \) is an arbitrary matrix in \( \mathrm{Sym}^+(n) \), we may compute \( D\pi(A)[\mathrm{Diag}(A)^{1/2}V\,\mathrm{Diag}(A)^{1/2}] \). It is like an inverse map of the natural projection and fortunately we have: \[ \mathrm{Diag}(\mathrm{Diag}(A)^{1/2}V\,\mathrm{Diag}(A)^{1/2}) = 0 \] This implies: \[ D\pi(A)[\mathrm{Diag}(A)^{1/2}V\,\mathrm{Diag}(A)^{1/2}] = D\,\mathrm{Diag}(A)^{1/2}V\,\mathrm{Diag}(A)^{1/2}D = V \] Therefore, \( \bar{V} := \mathrm{Diag}(A)^{1/2}V\,\mathrm{Diag}(A)^{1/2} \) is a well-defined lift of \( V \) at \( A \), but it does not belong to the horizontal space. The next step is to decompose this lift into two parts belonging to the vertical and horizontal spaces respectively. We write: \[ \bar{V} = AS + SA + K_V \] where \( AS + SA \in \mathcal{V}_A \) and \( K_V \in \mathcal{H}_A \). Using the condition of the horizontal space: \[ \mathrm{diag}(A^{-1}K_V) = \mathrm{diag}(A^{-1}\bar{V} - S - A^{-1}SA) = \mathrm{diag}(A^{-1}\bar{V}) - \mathrm{diag}(S) - \mathrm{diag}(A^{-1}SA) = 0 \] Let \( S = \mathrm{Diag}(s) \). Then: \[ (A^{-1}SA)_{ii} = \sum_{j=1}^n (A^{-1})_{ij}s_j A_{ij} = \sum_{j=1}^n (A^{-1})_{ij}A_{ij}s_j \] Therefore \( \mathrm{diag}(A^{-1}SA) = (A^{-1} \circ A)s \), where \( \circ \) is the elementwise product. So: \[ \mathrm{diag}(A^{-1}\bar{V}) = (\mathrm{Id} + (A^{-1} \circ A))s \implies s = (\mathrm{Id} + (A^{-1} \circ A))^{-1}\mathrm{diag}(A^{-1}\bar{V}) \] Therefore, the horizontal lift of \( V \) at \( A \) is explicitly: \[ \mathrm{lift}_A(V) = \bar{V} - AS - SA \] where \( \bar{V} = \mathrm{Diag}(A)^{1/2}V\,\mathrm{Diag}(A)^{1/2} \), \( S = \mathrm{Diag}(s) \) with \( s = (\mathrm{Id} + (A^{-1} \circ A))^{-1}\mathrm{diag}(A^{-1}\bar{V}) \).

Riemannian Metric, Gradient and Exponential Map

Let \( A \in \mathrm{Sym}^+(n) \) and \( U, V \in T_{\pi(A)}\mathrm{Cor}^+(n) \). We define the following inner product: \[ \langle U, V\rangle_{\pi(A)} = \langle \mathrm{lift}_A(U), \mathrm{lift}_A(V)\rangle_A \] To verify that this is a well-defined Riemannian metric, we need to check that if \( A \sim B \) (i.e. there exists \( D \in \mathrm{Diag}^+(n) \) such that \( A = DBD \)), then: \[ \langle \mathrm{lift}_A(U), \mathrm{lift}_A(V)\rangle_A = \langle \mathrm{lift}_B(U), \mathrm{lift}_B(V)\rangle_B \] Instead of proving this by hand, it is better to apply Theorem 9.38 in Boumal's book, which applies directly to our problem. In [David & Gu, 2019], they proved that \( \mathrm{Cor}^+(n) \cong \mathrm{Sym}^+(n)/\mathrm{Diag}^+(n) \) through the group action \( \phi : \mathrm{Diag}^+(n) \times \mathrm{Sym}^+(n) \to \mathrm{Sym}^+(n) \) defined by \( \phi(D, A) = DAD \), which acts smoothly, freely, and properly. To prove that this group action is isometric, we show that for all \( D \in \mathrm{Diag}^+(n) \), the map \( F(A) = DAD \) is isometric: \[ \forall\, A \in \mathrm{Cor}^+(n),\, \forall\, U, V \in T_A\mathrm{Cor}^+(n): \quad \langle DF(A)[U], DF(A)[V]\rangle_{F(A)} = \langle U, V\rangle_A \] In fact, \( DF(A)[U] = DUD \) for all \( U \in T_A\mathrm{Cor}^+(n) \), so: \begin{align*} \langle DF(A)[U], DF(A)[V]\rangle_{F(A)} &= \mathrm{Tr}((DAD)^{-1}DUD(DAD)^{-1}DVD) \\ &= \mathrm{Tr}(D^{-1}A^{-1}UA^{-1}VD) \\ &= \mathrm{Tr}(A^{-1}UA^{-1}V) = \langle U, V\rangle_A \end{align*} Together, we are ready to apply Theorem 9.38 and obtain that the above inner product is a Riemannian metric.

Now we derive an explicit form for the Riemannian metric. Since the metric is invariant in an equivalence class, we can suppose \( A \in \mathrm{Cor}^+(n) \), so the horizontal lift simplifies to: \[ \mathrm{lift}_A(V) = V - AS_V - S_VA \] where \( S_V = \mathrm{Diag}(s_V) \) and \( s_V = \Sigma^{-1}\mathrm{diag}(A^{-1}V) \), with \( \Sigma = \mathrm{Id} + A^{-1} \circ A \).

Key observation. Let \( U, V \in T_A\mathrm{Cor}^+(n) \). Since \( \mathrm{lift}_A(\cdot) \) belongs to the horizontal space and \( V - \mathrm{lift}_A(V) \) is in the vertical space: \[ \langle \mathrm{lift}_A(U),\, V - \mathrm{lift}_A(V)\rangle_A = 0 \implies \langle \mathrm{lift}_A(U), \mathrm{lift}_A(V)\rangle_A = \langle \mathrm{lift}_A(U), V\rangle_A \]

We continue the computation:

\[ \langle \mathrm{lift}_A(U), V\rangle_A = \mathrm{Tr}(A^{-1}VA^{-1}(U - AS_U - S_UA)) = \mathrm{Tr}(A^{-1}VA^{-1}U) - \mathrm{Tr}(A^{-1}VA^{-1}(AS_U + S_UA)) \]

Note that \( \mathrm{Tr}(A^{-1}VA^{-1}AS_U) = \mathrm{Tr}(A^{-1}VS_U) = \mathrm{Tr}(VA^{-1}S_U) = \mathrm{Tr}(A^{-1}VA^{-1}S_UA) \). This follows from the cyclic and product properties of the trace. Therefore:

\begin{align*} \langle \mathrm{lift}_A(U), V\rangle_A &= \mathrm{Tr}(A^{-1}VA^{-1}U) - 2\mathrm{Tr}(A^{-1}VS_U) \\ &= \mathrm{Tr}(A^{-1}VA^{-1}U) - 2\,\mathrm{diag}(A^{-1}V)^\top S_U \\ &= \mathrm{Tr}(A^{-1}VA^{-1}U) - 2\,\mathrm{diag}(A^{-1}V)^\top \Sigma^{-1}\mathrm{diag}(A^{-1}U) \\ &= \langle U, V\rangle_A - 2\,\mathrm{diag}(A^{-1}V)^\top \Sigma^{-1}\mathrm{diag}(A^{-1}U) \end{align*}

Riemannian gradient. Let \( f : \mathrm{Cor}^+(n) \to \mathbb{R} \) be a smooth function. Define its lift: \[ \bar{f} = f \circ \pi : \mathrm{Sym}^+(n) \to \mathbb{R} \] Let \( A \in \mathrm{Sym}^+(n) \) and \( V \in T_A\mathrm{Cor}^+(n) \). By Proposition 9.39: \[ \mathrm{lift}_A(\mathrm{grad}\, f(\pi(A))) = \mathrm{grad}\, \bar{f}(A) \] Applying \( D\pi(A) \) to both sides: \[ \mathrm{grad}\, f(\pi(A)) = D\pi(A)[\mathrm{grad}\, \bar{f}(A)] \] Without loss of generality, suppose \( A \in \mathrm{Cor}^+(n) \). Then: \[ \mathrm{grad}\, f(A) = \mathrm{grad}\, \bar{f}(A) - \tfrac{1}{2}\mathrm{Diag}(\mathrm{grad}\, \bar{f}(A))\, A - \tfrac{1}{2}A\, \mathrm{Diag}(\mathrm{grad}\, \bar{f}(A)) \]

Exponential map. A potential candidate specified in Boumal's book is, for \( A \in \mathrm{Sym}^+(n) \) and \( V \in T_{\pi(A)}\mathrm{Cor}^+(n) \): \[ R_{\pi(A)}(V) = \pi(\bar{R}_A(\mathrm{lift}_A(V))) \] where \( \bar{R} \) is the retraction (often chosen as the exponential map) of \( \mathrm{Sym}^+(n) \). Since our objective is to work in \( \mathrm{Cor}^+(n) \), we can suppose \( A \in \mathrm{Cor}^+(n) \) to simplify computations. In this case: \[ R_A(V) = \pi(\bar{R}_A(\mathrm{lift}_A(V))) = \pi(\bar{R}_A(V - AS_V - S_VA)) \] The retraction for \( \mathrm{Sym}^+(n) \) is chosen as: \[ \bar{R}_A(U) = \mathrm{Exp}_A(U) = A^{1/2}\exp(A^{-1/2}UA^{-1/2})A^{1/2} \] Replacing \( U \) by \( V - AS_V - S_VA \): \[ R_A(V) = \pi\!\left(A^{1/2}\exp\!\left(A^{-1/2}(V - S_VA - AS_V)A^{-1/2}\right)A^{1/2}\right) \] This is also an exponential map corresponding to the Riemannian metric. We can check that \( R_A(0) = A \) and: \begin{align*} DR_A(0)[V] &= D\pi(\mathrm{Exp}_A(0))[D\mathrm{Exp}_A(0)[D(\mathrm{lift}_A(0))[V]]] \\ &= D\pi(A)[D\mathrm{Exp}_A(0)[\mathrm{lift}_A(V)]] \\ &= D\pi(A)[\mathrm{lift}_A(V)] = V \end{align*}

Conclusion

Together, we derived the necessary formulas for the space of SPD matrices with unit diagonal. In brief, let \( A \in \mathrm{Cor}^+(n) \), \( U, V \in T_A\mathrm{Cor}^+(n) \), and \( \Sigma = \mathrm{Id} + A^{-1} \circ A \):

Summary
  • Riemannian metric: \( \langle U, V\rangle_A = \mathrm{Tr}(A^{-1}UA^{-1}V) - 2\,\mathrm{diag}(A^{-1}U)^\top \Sigma^{-1}\mathrm{diag}(A^{-1}V) \)
  • Riemannian gradient: \( \mathrm{grad}\, f(A) = \mathrm{grad}\, \bar{f}(A) - \frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Diag}(\mathrm{grad}\, \bar{f}(A))\,A - \frac{1}{2}A\,\mathrm{Diag}(\mathrm{grad}\, \bar{f}(A)) \), where \( \bar{f} = f \circ \pi \)
  • Exponential map: \( R_A(V) = \pi\!\left(A^{1/2}\exp\!\left(A^{-1/2}(V - S_VA - AS_V)A^{-1/2}\right)A^{1/2}\right) \)

This is compatible with the work of [Thanwerdas & Pennec, 2022], especially for the Riemannian metric and the exponential map.
