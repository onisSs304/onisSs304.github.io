some ideas on analyzing momentum-based methods via large deviation theory (part 1)

Introduction

Based on 2 interesting works on exploring Stochastic gradient descent (SGD) via large deviation principles (LDP) ([Azizian et al., 2024], [Azizian et al., 2025]), I will try to extend this theoretical framework to some momentum-based SGD. During this post, 2 approaches will be introduced to analyze the LDP for SGD with momentum (SGDM). The standard iteration for SGDM is given by: \[ \begin{cases} x_{n+1} = x_n + v_{n+1} \\ v_{n+1} = \beta v_n - \eta \nabla G(x_n, \omega_n) \end{cases} \] where \( G(x_n, \omega_n) \) is a stochastic estimate of the gradient of the objective which is assumed to be smooth; \( \beta \) is the momentum decay and \( \eta \) is the learning rate. We may write that: \[ G(x_n, \omega_n) = \nabla f(x_n) - U(x_n, \omega_n) \] with \( \mathbb{E}[U(x_n, \omega_n)] = 0 \) and \( \mathrm{Cov}(U(x_n, \omega_n)) \succ 0 \), which are standard assumptions for SGD.

Continuous-time form of SGDM. Inspired by the spirit of [Azizian et al., 2024], we provide a continuous-time form for SGDM in order to analyze LDP. First, we suppose that the iterations of SGDM are a discretization of a system of 2 ODEs \( (X_t, V_t) \) with time rescaling \( \Delta t = \eta \). We have that: \[ x_{n+1} - x_n = v_{n+1} \iff \frac{x_{n+1} - x_n}{\eta} = \frac{v_{n+1}}{\eta} \implies \dot{X}_t = \frac{V_t}{\eta} \] Similarly: \[ \frac{v_{n+1} - v_n}{\eta} = -\frac{1-\beta}{\eta}v_n - G(x_n, \omega_n) \implies \dot{V}_t = -\frac{1-\beta}{\eta}V_t - G(X_t, \omega_{\lfloor t/\eta \rfloor}) \] Finally, we get: \[ \begin{cases} \dot{X}_t = \dfrac{V_t}{\eta} \\[6pt] \dot{V}_t = -\dfrac{1-\beta}{\eta}V_t - G(X_t, \omega_{\lfloor t/\eta \rfloor}) \end{cases} \] We may simplify this system by setting \( W_t = V_t / \eta \) and obtain: \[ \begin{cases} \dot{X}_t = W_t \\[6pt] \dot{W}_t = \dfrac{1}{\eta}\left(-(1-\beta)W_t - G(X_t, \omega_{\lfloor t/\eta \rfloor})\right) \end{cases} \tag{3} \]

Large deviation theory. This is a very vast field in mathematics; we cannot cover all of it in such a small post. I only recall a definition that I find easy to follow. Let \( (P_\epsilon)_\epsilon \) be a family of probability measures on \( (\mathcal{X}, \mathcal{B}) \). Let \( I \) be a good rate function if it is a lower semicontinuous mapping \( \mathcal{X} \to (0, +\infty) \) and the level sets \( \{x \mid I(x) \leq a\} \) are compact subsets of \( \mathcal{X} \).

Definition — Large Deviation Principle
\( \{P_\epsilon\} \) satisfies the large deviation principle with a rate function if for all \( A \in \mathcal{B} \): \[ -\inf_{x \in A^o} I(x) \leq \lim_{\epsilon \to 0}\inf\, \epsilon \log P_\epsilon(A) \leq \lim_{\epsilon \to 0}\sup\, \epsilon \log P_\epsilon(A) \leq -\inf_{x \in \bar{A}} I(x) \] where \( A^o \) is the interior of \( A \) (the largest open subset of \( A \)) and \( \bar{A} \) is the closure of \( A \) (the smallest closed set containing \( A \)). It may cause some confusion; I often think informally that: \[ \lim_{\epsilon \to 0} P_\epsilon(A) \approx \exp\!\left(-\frac{I(x)}{\epsilon}\right) \]

In our situation, we study the trajectories of a random process described by the iterates of SGD(M). Therefore, the problem we will deal with has the form: given a trajectory, we study the probability that SGD(M) follows this trajectory. In [Azizian et al., 2024], the path of SGD is given by an interpolated continuous sequence and the space of trajectories is given by the space of differentiable, continuous functions. They adapted the theory of [Freidlin & Wentzell, 1998] and the further analysis for SGDM will be based on their theoretical framework.

Reduced-ODE to Approximate Path of SGDM

As mentioned in (3), we set \( b_1(x,y) = y \) and \( b_2(x,y) = -(1-\beta)y - G(x, \omega) \), then obtain an equivalent form: \[ \begin{cases} \dot{X}_t = b_1(X_t, W_t) \\ \dot{W}_t = \eta^{-1} b_2(X_t, W_t) \end{cases} \tag{4} \] The variable \( W_t \) is called the fast variable due to the term \( \eta^{-1} \), and \( X_t \) is the slow variable. Inspired by some standard results for averaging principles for ODE systems in [Pavliotis & Stuart, 2008] and Section 1, Chap. 7 of [Freidlin & Wentzell, 1998], we may approximate \( X_t \) by an ODE under some restrictive assumptions.

We assume that both functions \( b_1, b_2 \) are bounded and continuously differentiable. We consider the fast motion \( W_t(x) \) for fixed slow variable \( x \in \mathbb{R}^d \): \[ \dot{W}_t(x) = b_2(x, W_t(x)) = -(1-\beta)W_t(x) - G(x, \omega) \] Solving this simple ODE: \[ W_t(x) = W_0(x)e^{-(1-\beta)t} - \frac{G(x,\omega)}{1-\beta} \] Then we prove that the following limit exists: \[ \lim_{T \to \infty} \frac{1}{T} \int_0^T b_1(x, W_s(x))\, ds = \bar{b}_1(x) \] Indeed: \[ \int_0^T b_1(x, W_s(x))\, ds = \int_0^T W_s(x)\, ds = -\frac{W_0(x)}{1-\beta}e^{-(1-\beta)T} - \frac{T \cdot G(x,\omega)}{1-\beta} + \frac{W_0(x)}{1-\beta} \] Therefore: \[ \bar{b}_1(x) = \lim_{T \to \infty} \frac{1}{T}\left(-\frac{W_0(x)}{1-\beta}e^{-(1-\beta)T} - \frac{T \cdot G(x,\omega)}{1-\beta} + \frac{W_0(x)}{1-\beta}\right) = -\frac{G(x,\omega)}{1-\beta} \] Therefore, under some assumptions, the process \( X_t \) may be approximated by the solution of the following ODE: \[ \dot{x}(t) = \bar{b}_1(x(t)) = -\frac{G(x_t, \omega)}{1-\beta} \] The proof can be found in Chapter 8 of [Pavliotis & Stuart, 2008]. Now, all ingredients are gathered. To obtain the LDP for SGDM, we reuse the framework of [Azizian et al., 2024]:

The discrete "rescaled" trajectory: \( x^\eta_n = x_{n\lfloor 1/\eta \rfloor} \)

The continuous "interpolated" trajectory: for any \( n \geq 0 \) and \( t \in [n\eta, (n+1)\eta] \): \[ X_t = x_n + \left(\frac{t}{\eta} - n\right)(x_{n+1} - x_n) \]

The continuous "discretized noise" trajectory: \( Z_0 = x_0 \) and for any \( t > 0 \): \[ \dot{Z}_t = -\frac{1}{1-\beta}\left(\nabla f(Z_t) - U(Z_t, \omega_{\lfloor t/\eta \rfloor})\right) \]

As in the original work, we will obtain the LDP for \( Z_t \) then transfer to \( X_t \) and finally \( x^\eta_n \). Actually, obtaining the LDP for \( Z_t \) is straightforward based on the theory in [Freidlin & Wentzell, 1998], so the main challenge is to prove that: \[ \mathrm{dist}_{[0,T]}(X, Z) \leq c\eta \] where \( c \) depends on many other parameters and \( \mathrm{dist}_{[0,T]}(X, Z) = \sup_{t \in [0,T]}\|X_t - Z_t\| \). Furthermore, we also reuse the blanket assumptions of the objective and the noise as in the original work. Let us state an informal lemma (since I have not verified all the necessary assumptions):

Lemma 1
Fix \( \mathcal{K} \) a compact subset, \( T > 0 \), \( \eta_0 > 0 \), and \( Z_t \) at least twice differentiable. There exists a constant \( c = c(T, \mathcal{K}, f, U, \eta_0) \) such that: \[ \mathrm{dist}_{[0,T]}(X, Z) \leq c\eta \]

Note that the proof is not trivially the same as Lemma C.2 of [Azizian et al., 2024] due to the difference in the dynamics of \( (Z_t)_{t \in [0,T]} \) and the iterates of SGDM. Motivated by the proof of Theorem 3 of [Kovachki & Stuart, 2020], we proceed the proof of our lemma by the following steps: prove the pointwise boundedness at \( (n\eta)_n \) for the distance, then use the interpolation property of \( X_t \) to extend the boundedness to the remaining points. Suppose that \( T = N\eta \). The first step is already done in [Kovachki & Stuart, 2020] (with some modifications due to the noise added), i.e. there exists \( c_1 \) such that: \[ \mathrm{dist}_{0 \leq n\eta \leq T}(X(n), Z(n)) \leq c_1\eta \] where \( X(n) = x_n \) and \( Z(n) = Z_{n\eta} \). Now denote \( X(n+\epsilon) = X_{(n+\epsilon)\eta} = \epsilon X(n+1) + (1-\epsilon)X(n) \) and \( Z(n+\epsilon) = Z_{(n+\epsilon)\eta} \) where \( \epsilon \in [0,1] \). By Taylor expansion: \[ Z(n+\epsilon) = Z(n) + \epsilon\eta\dot{Z}(n) + M_n \] Since \( Z_t \) is twice differentiable, we can specify the remainder \( M_n \) by: \[ M_n = \int_{n\eta}^{(n+\epsilon)\eta} \ddot{Z}(t)\,((n+\epsilon)\eta - t)\,dt = \eta\int_0^\epsilon \ddot{Z}((n+s)\eta)(\epsilon - s)\,ds \] Similarly, we can get the expansion for \( Z(n+\epsilon) \) via \( Z(n+1) \): \[ Z(n+\epsilon) = Z(n+1) - (1-\epsilon)\eta\dot{Z}(n+1) + \eta\int_1^\epsilon \ddot{Z}((n+s)\eta)(\epsilon - s)\,ds \] Multiplying the first expression by \( 1-\epsilon \) and the second by \( \epsilon \), then summing the two terms: \[ Z(n+\epsilon) = (1-\epsilon)Z(n) + \epsilon Z(n+1) + \eta\epsilon(1-\epsilon)(\dot{Z}(n) - \dot{Z}(n+1)) + \eta((1-\epsilon)M_n + \epsilon M_{n+1}) \] Taking the difference between \( Z(n+\epsilon) \) and \( X(n+\epsilon) \) and using the triangle inequality (note that \( e_n = \|Z(n) - X(n)\| \leq c_1\eta \)): \[ \|Z(n+\epsilon) - X(n+\epsilon)\| \leq (1-\epsilon)e_n + \epsilon e_{n+1} + \eta\left(\|\dot{Z}(n)\| + \|\dot{Z}(n+1)\| + \|M_n\| + \|M_{n+1}\|\right) \] Since we only work on a finite time interval, \( Z_t \) is bounded uniformly on \( [0,T] \), and so are \( \dot{Z}_t \) and \( \ddot{Z}_t \). That implies a bound for \( \|\dot{Z}(n)\| + \|\dot{Z}(n+1)\| + \|M_n\| + \|M_{n+1}\| \) which depends on \( T \); denote it \( c_2 \). Finally, we get \( \mathrm{dist}_{[0,T]}(X,Z) \leq (c_1 + c_2)\eta \).

Before deducing the LDP for \( X_t \), we need to recalculate the Lagrangian and then the action functional for the process \( Z_t \). By using the first-order ODE, we avoid the issue arising in the degenerate case (3). Let \( b(x,\omega) = -\frac{1}{1-\beta}(\nabla f(x) - U(x,\omega)) \). Following the theory highlighted in Chapter 7.4 of [Freidlin & Wentzell, 1998], we first deduce the LDP for the process \( Z_t \). The cumulant generating function is: \[ H^M(x,v) = \log\mathbb{E}[\exp(\langle v, b(x,\omega)\rangle)] \] where \( \langle v, b(x,\omega)\rangle = -\frac{1}{1-\beta}\langle v, \nabla f(x)\rangle + \left\langle\frac{v}{1-\beta}, U(x,\omega)\right\rangle \). Let \( \bar{H}(x,v) = \log\mathbb{E}[\exp(\langle v, U(x,\omega)\rangle)] \). Then: \[ H^M(x,v) = \bar{H}\!\left(x, \frac{v}{1-\beta}\right) - \left\langle\frac{v}{1-\beta}, \nabla f(x)\right\rangle \] We define the Lagrangians: \begin{align*} \bar{L}(x,v) &= \bar{H}(x,\cdot)^*(v) = \sup_p\{\langle p, v\rangle - \bar{H}(x,p)\} \\ L^M(x,v) &= H^M(x,\cdot)^*(v) = \sup_p\left\{\langle p, v\rangle - \left(\bar{H}\!\left(x,\frac{p}{1-\beta}\right) - \left\langle\frac{p}{1-\beta}, \nabla f(x)\right\rangle\right)\right\} \end{align*} By simplifying: \[ L^M(x,v) = \sup_p\left\{\left\langle\frac{\nabla f(x)}{1-\beta} + v,\, p\right\rangle - \bar{H}\!\left(x, \frac{p}{1-\beta}\right)\right\} = \bar{L}(x,\, v(1-\beta) + \nabla f(x)) \] Then the action functional is \( S^M_{[0,T]}(\phi) = \int_0^T L^M(\phi(t), \dot{\phi}(t))\,dt \) if \( \phi \) is absolutely continuous, and \( +\infty \) otherwise.

Some comparison to Vanilla SGD (SGDV). The Lagrangian and action functional will be denoted by \( L^V(x,v) \) and \( S^V_{[0,T]}(\phi) \). Note that \( \bar{L}(x,v) \) is the same for both SGDV and SGDM. At first sight, we may realize that: \[ L^M(x,v) = L^V(x,(1-\beta)v) \quad \text{and} \quad S^M_{[0,T]}(\phi) = \int_0^T L^V(\phi(t),(1-\beta)\dot{\phi}(t))\,dt \]

Informal results. From Lemma 1 as the foundation, we follow the same steps in Appendix C of [Azizian et al., 2024] to deduce the LDP for the accelerated sequence \( (x^\eta_n)_n \).

As in the definition of the large deviation principle stated above, we need 2 properties to have a good rate function: compactness of the level sets and lower semicontinuity of the action functional. Indeed, this holds for sure by extending the proof in the original work. From that, we could deduce the LDP for the interpolated process \( X_t \):

Lemma 2
Fix \( T > 0 \). For any \( \delta, \varepsilon \geq 0 \), \( \mathcal{K} \subset \mathcal{X} \) compact, there exists \( \eta_0 > 0 \) such that for any \( \eta \in (0, \eta_0] \) and any \( x_0 \in \mathcal{K} \): \begin{align*} \mathbb{P}_{x_0}\!\left(\mathrm{dist}_{[0,T]}(X,\gamma) < \delta\right) &\geq \exp\!\left(-\frac{\mathcal{S}^M_{[0,T]}(\gamma) + \varepsilon}{\eta}\right) \\ \mathbb{P}_{x_0}\!\left(\mathrm{dist}_{[0,T]}\!\left(X, \Gamma_{[0,T]}^{\{x_0\}}(s)\right)> \delta\right) &< \exp\!\left(-\frac{s - \varepsilon}{\eta}\right) \end{align*} for all \( \gamma \in \Gamma_{[0,T]}^{\{x_0\}}(s) :=\{\gamma \in C([0,T], \mathcal{X}) : \gamma_0=x_0,\, S_{[0,T]}(\gamma) \leq s\} \).

Next, we obtain the LDP for the accelerated sequence. Let \( x, y \in \mathcal{X}^\mathbb{N} \), and define the metric: \[ \mathrm{dist}_T(x,y) = \max_{0 \leq n \leq N-1}\|x_n - y_n\| \] To connect the LDP for \( x^\eta_n \) from the continuous interpolated process \( X_t \), we need to bound the difference between the discrete sequence and the interpolation. We yield the same conclusion as Lemma C.4 of [Azizian et al., 2024]: for any \( \eta_0 \geq 0 \) and \( N \geq 1 \), fix \( \mathcal{K} \subset \mathcal{X} \). Then there exists a constant \( c = c(\mathcal{K}, \eta_0, N, f) \) such that for any \( x_0 \in \mathcal{K} \) and \( \eta \in [0, \eta_0] \): \[ \mathrm{dist}_N(x^\eta, (X_n)_{0 \leq n \leq N-1}) = \max_{0 \leq n \leq N-1}\|x_n^\eta - X_n\| \leq c\eta \] Then we mimic the next steps in this section and get the LDP for the rescaled sequence \( (x^\eta_n)_n \).

Discussion. The key role is played by Lemma 1, from which we can refine the analysis and possibly deduce the LDP for the discrete rescaled sequence. However, this may suffer some limitations caused by the fact that we obtained the LDP via a reduced first-order ODE. This may help us avoid complicated computations due to degenerate effects and take advantage of the available base theories of [Azizian et al., 2024]. I believe that obtaining the LDP directly from the system (3) is better and would generally enrich the theories of large deviations in SGD. We will discuss this in the next section (or next post).

Invariant measure. We associate a certain transition cost to each pair of critical points. First, we define the quasi-potential between 2 points \( x, x' \in \mathcal{X} \): \[ B(x,x') = \inf\{S_T[\gamma] : \gamma \in C_T(x,x'),\, T \in \mathbb{N}\} \] Then the corresponding quasi-potential between 2 sets is: \[ B(K,K') = \inf\{B(x,x') : x \in K,\, x' \in K'\} \] Let us inject our situation. We had that: \[ S^M_T(\phi) = \int_0^T L^V(\phi(t),(1-\beta)\dot{\phi}(t))\,dt = (1-\beta)\int_0^{T/(1-\beta)} L^V(\phi(\tau(1-\beta)),\,\dot{\phi}(\tau(1-\beta)))\,d\tau = (1-\beta)S^V_{T/(1-\beta)}(\gamma) \] where \( \gamma(t) = \phi(t(1-\beta)) \). Then the quasi-potential of the 2 variants of SGD are related by: \[ B^M(x,x') \approx (1-\beta)B^V(x,x') \] The approximation appears in this relationship because we define the quasi-potential as the infimum over finite time. For the energy of the component \( K_i \), it is simply the energy of SGDV scaled by \( 1-\beta \): \[ E^M_i \approx (1-\beta)E^V_i, \quad \forall\, i \] Therefore, the invariant measure of SGD with momentum in a critical component of \( f \) is defined informally as: \[ \mu^M(\mathcal{U}_i) \propto \exp\!\left(-\frac{E^M_i}{\eta}\right) \approx \exp\!\left(-\frac{(1-\beta)E^V_i}{\eta}\right) \propto \mu^V(\mathcal{U}_i)^{1-\beta} \]

Conclusion. From the previous expression on the invariant measure of the iterates of SGD with momentum, we can state that after a long run, the iterates of SGDM concentrate more heavily than SGDV on stable minima of the objective. As stated in [Azizian et al., 2024], the long-run behaviour of SGD tends to avoid saddle points (unstable minima), and so does SGDM. From that point, we could conclude that the iterates of SGDM tend to find local minima more quickly than vanilla SGD. However, we cannot recover another well-known result of momentum methods: better avoidance of saddle points or faster exit from a local minimum, which would be equivalent to finding global minima more quickly.
