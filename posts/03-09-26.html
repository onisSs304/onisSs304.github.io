some ideas on analyzing momentum-based methods via large deviation theory (part 3)

Assumptions

Following the previous posts, we will introduce some necessary assumptions to be able to obtain the LDP for the iterates of Stochastic Gradient Descent with Momentum. Specifically, we need a clean handle for regularity, boundedness for the loss, compactness for the iterates, and probably the information of the noise. However, the details for the LDP proof will not be presented here since they already appear in several earlier works [Boué et al., 2000], [Dupuis, 1988], or [Gulinsky & Veretennikov, 1993].

First, we recall the iterates of SGDM:

\[ \begin{cases} x_{n+1} = x_n + \eta v_{n+1} \\ v_{n+1} = \beta v_n - \nabla G(x_n, \omega) = \beta v_n - \nabla f(x_n) + U(x_n, \omega_n) \\ x_0, v_0 \in \mathbb{R}^d \end{cases} \tag{1} \]

Loss objective. We reuse the same assumptions as in [Azizian et al., 2024]:

  • Coercivity: \( f(x) \to \infty \) as \( \|x\| \to \infty \).
  • Smoothness: \( f \) is \( C^2 \)-differentiable and its gradient is \( L \)-Lipschitz continuous, that is: \[ \|\nabla f(x') - \nabla f(x)\| \leq L\|x - x'\| \]
  • Critical set regularity: the critical set \( \mathrm{crit}\, f := \{x \in \mathcal{X} : \nabla f(x) = 0\} \) consists of a finite number of smoothly connected components \( \mathcal{K}_i \).

Information of the noise.

  • \( \mathbb{E}[U(x,\omega)] = 0 \), \( \mathrm{Cov}(U(x,\omega)) \succ 0 \), and the covariance is continuous with respect to \( x \).
  • Smooth growth: \( U(x,\omega) \) is \( C^2 \)-differentiable and satisfies the growth condition: \[ \sup_{x,\omega} \frac{\|U(x,\omega)\|}{1 + \|x\|} < \infty \]
  • This is the different point from the original work: we need a transition kernel condition for the Markov chain \( v_n^x \) defined by: \[ v_{n+1}^x = \beta v_n^x - \nabla f(x) + U(x, \omega_n) \tag{2} \] The transition kernel has a density with respect to a fixed measure \( \lambda \), defined by \( P^x(v, dv') = p^x(v, v')\lambda(dv') \). Inspired by [Dupuis, 1988] and [Boué et al., 2000], the sufficient condition should be: there exist \( 0 < a \leq A < \infty \) such that, given a compact set \( F \) and \( x \) belonging to it, we have \( a \leq p^x(v, v') \leq A \), and \( p^x(v, v') \) is continuous in \( x \), uniformly in \( v, v' \) in a compact set.

Discussion of assumptions. Except for the transition kernel condition, all remaining assumptions are already considered carefully in [Azizian et al., 2024]. Therefore, we should take a careful look at the transition kernel. Suppose the fixed measure \( \lambda \) is the Lebesgue measure. Note that the above condition is stated in a general form; we will now go into the details for SGDM. Given the Markov chain (2) and a frozen \( x \), the transition probability is: \[ P^x(v, dv') = \mathbb{P}(\beta v - \nabla f(x) + U(x,\omega) \in dv') = f_x(v' + \nabla f(x) - \beta v)\, dv' \] where \( U(x,\omega) \) has a density \( f_x(\cdot) \) with respect to the Lebesgue measure. Then \( p^x(v,v') = f_x(v' + \nabla f(x) - \beta v) \). Given compact sets \( E, F \) and the continuity of \( \nabla f \), for all \( x \in F \) and \( v, v' \in E \), the quantity \( v' + \nabla f(x) - \beta v \) also belongs to a compact set. To guarantee that \( f_x(v' + \nabla f(x) - \beta v) \) is always positive on every compact set, we consider some types of distribution for \( U(x,\omega) \):

  • Discrete distribution: the state space is finite and the density may be zero at some points.
  • Globally continuous distribution: the density is nonzero and continuous at all points; this type of distribution essentially satisfies our condition.
  • Bounded continuous distribution such as the Uniform law or Truncated Gaussian: this may be more complicated. If we can prove that the iterates \( x_n \) and \( v_n \) belong to a compact set (which should be true), the domain of the distribution should be large enough to cover \( \{v' + \nabla f(x) - \beta v : x, v, v' \text{ belong to a certain compact set}\} \).
Compactness of the Iterates
Lemma — Growth of \( x_n \)
For any \( x_0 \in \mathcal{X} = \mathbb{R}^d \), \( \eta_0 > 0 \), for every \( \eta \in (0, \eta_0) \), for any \( N \geq 1 \) and \( 0 \leq n \leq N(\lfloor\eta^{-1}\rfloor + 1) \), there exist constants \( c_1(x_0, x_1, \eta, \beta) \) and \( c_2(x_0, x_1, \eta, \beta) \) such that: \[ \|x_n\| \leq c_1 + c_2 \exp\!\left(\frac{2MN(1+\eta_0)}{2+\beta}\right)({\beta + 2})^n \]

This is the best bound I could find; the proof is based on solving an inequality recursion problem. Let me recall a simple version of SGDM: \[ \begin{cases} x_{n+1} = x_n + \beta(x_n - x_{n-1}) - \eta\nabla f(x_n) + U(x_n, \omega_n) \\ x_0,\quad x_1 = x_0 - \eta\nabla f(x_0) \end{cases} \tag{3} \] The assumptions above imply the existence of a positive number \( M \) such that: \[ \|\nabla f(x)\| \leq M(1+\|x\|), \quad \|U(x,\omega)\| \leq M(1+\|x\|), \quad \forall\, x \in \mathcal{X} \] The second inequality comes from the growth condition of the noise, while the first is deduced from the \( L \)-Lipschitz gradient condition. Taking the norm and using the triangle inequality: \begin{align*} \|x_{n+1}\| &\leq (1+\beta)\|x_n\| + \beta\|x_{n-1}\| + \eta(\|\nabla f(x_n)\| + \|U(x_n, \omega_n)\|) \\ &\leq (1+\beta+2M\eta)\|x_n\| + \beta\|x_{n-1}\| + 2M\eta \end{align*} Let \( y_n = \|x_n\| + \dfrac{M\eta}{\beta + M\eta} \), then \( y_{n+1} \leq (1+\beta+2M\eta)y_n + \beta y_{n-1} \). One can show that: \[ y_n \leq ap^n + bq^n \] where \( pq = -\beta \) and \( p + q = 1 + \beta + 2M\eta > 1 \). Since \( 0 \leq \beta < 1 \), we can assume \( -1 < p < -\beta \) and \( q> 1 \). The exact form of \( a \) and \( b \) will depend on the initial conditions and parameters, specifically \( x_0, x_1, \beta, \eta \). Then: \begin{align*} y_n &\leq |a| + |b| \cdot q^n = |a| + |b|(1+\beta+2M\eta-p)^n \leq |a| + |b|(2+\beta+2M\eta)^n \\ &= |a| + |b| \cdot (2+\beta)^n\left(1 + \frac{2M\eta}{2+\beta}\right)^n \leq |a| + |b| \cdot (2+\beta)^n \exp\!\left(\frac{2M\eta n}{2+\beta}\right) \\ &\leq |a| + |b| \cdot (2+\beta)^n \exp\!\left(\frac{2MN(1+\eta)}{2+\beta}\right) \end{align*} This concludes the lemma. It shows that in finite time and for every constant \( \eta \), the iterates \( x_n \) belong to a compact set. However, I think that this bound can be improved since the term \( (2+\beta)^n \) is quite dangerous.

Now suppose that for \( N \geq 1 \), the sequence \( (x_n)_{n \leq N} \) lies in a compact set, i.e. its norm is bounded by some positive number. We have: \[ \|v_{n+1}\| \leq \beta\|v_n\| + \|\nabla f(x_n)\| + \|U(x_n, \omega_n)\| \leq \beta\|v_n\| + 2M(1+\|x_n\|) \leq \beta\|v_n\| + K \] We can solve this recursion easily and prove that \( (v_n)_{n \leq N} \) lies in a ball with radius depending on the boundedness of \( (x_n)_{n \leq N} \).

Large Deviation Principle

The existence of the Hamiltonian. Following the work of [Dupuis, 1988] and [Iscoe, Ney & Nummelin, 1985], the assumptions above and the compactness of the iterates \( (x_n) \) and \( (v_n) \) in finite time ensure the existence of the Hamiltonian stated in the previous post.

Laplace principle. Then by the proof in [Boué et al., 2000], we can conclude that the continuous interpolated, piecewise linear process formed by \( (x_n)_{n \leq N} \) satisfies the Laplace principle (also stated in the previous post). Furthermore, the iterates \( (x_n)_{n \leq N} \) act on a compact set, which is essentially a Polish space. So the Laplace principle is equivalent to the Large Deviation Principle.
